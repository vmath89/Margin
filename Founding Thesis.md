# **Founding Thesis**

## **The core belief**

**The future of reading is not passive consumption. It is conversation.**

Books, papers, essays, reports, and other long-form content are still consumed using an interface designed around a centuries-old assumption: the author speaks and the reader tries to understand.

AI changes that relationship.

For the first time, a reader can stop at any moment and say:

**“I don't understand this.”**  
**“Explain this differently.”**  
**“Go deeper.”**  
**“Challenge this argument.”**  
**“How does this connect to something I read before?”**

And receive an intelligent answer immediately.

We believe this will create a new category between **reading, audiobooks, and tutoring**: an AI-native medium where people learn through an ongoing conversation with what they consume.

---

# **The problem**

There is more high-quality knowledge available than ever before, but consuming difficult material remains unnecessarily hard.

People abandon books because:

* they lose concentration;  
* difficult passages create friction;  
* they lack the necessary background knowledge;  
* they forget earlier concepts;  
* they don't have someone available to discuss ideas with;  
* reading requires dedicated screen time;  
* audiobooks solve convenience but not comprehension.

AI products have started addressing pieces of this problem, but the experience remains fragmented.

A user today might:

1. read a book in Kindle;  
2. encounter something confusing;  
3. open ChatGPT;  
4. type or paste a passage;  
5. ask for an explanation;  
6. read the explanation;  
7. return to the book;  
8. later switch to an audiobook while walking.

The intelligence exists.

The voice technology exists.

The content exists.

**What is missing is the product that combines them into one continuous learning experience.**

---

# **Our thesis**

We believe people will increasingly **listen to and converse with knowledge rather than simply read it.**

The winning product will not merely convert text into speech.

It will:

**understand the source, understand the reader, explain the material, hold a conversation about it, and then seamlessly continue.**

The fundamental interaction becomes:

**Listen → Interrupt → Understand → Discuss → Continue.**

A person walking to work could listen to a chapter of a philosophy book.

They say:

> “Stop. I don't understand why Seneca believes this.”

The narration stops.

The AI explains the argument using the surrounding chapter and the reader's existing level of understanding.

The reader challenges it.

The AI responds.

The reader says:

> “Okay, continue.”

The book resumes from exactly where it stopped.

That experience should feel as natural as having an extraordinarily knowledgeable tutor sitting beside you while you read.

---

# **Why now**

Several technologies have crossed the necessary threshold simultaneously.

### **1\. Frontier models can genuinely explain difficult material**

Modern models can reason about philosophy, technical papers, economics, history, business, and other complex subjects at a level where conversations can meaningfully improve understanding.

### **2\. Synthetic speech has become good enough for long-form listening**

Generated voices are increasingly natural enough that listening to AI-generated explanations no longer feels like listening to traditional text-to-speech.

### **3\. Speech interaction has become fast enough**

Users can interrupt, ask questions naturally, and continue without needing to type.

### **4\. Long-context models and retrieval allow persistent understanding**

A system can understand not only the current paragraph but the chapter, book, previously read material, annotations, and past conversations.

The technological primitives already exist.

**The remaining problem is primarily product design.**

---

# **The initial wedge**

We should not attempt to replace Kindle, Audible, or every form of reading.

We start where our advantage is greatest:

## **Difficult nonfiction**

Examples:

* philosophy;  
* history;  
* economics;  
* science;  
* business;  
* biographies;  
* textbooks;  
* research papers;  
* long essays.

For entertainment-driven fiction, traditional reading and audiobooks already work extremely well.

For knowledge-dense nonfiction, however, **understanding is part of the product**.

That is where an intelligent reading companion creates dramatically more value than ordinary narration.

---

# **The user**

Our first user is not someone who doesn't want to read.

It is someone who **wants to understand more than they currently have time or energy to understand.**

They:

* buy books they genuinely want to finish;  
* listen to podcasts and audiobooks;  
* save long articles they never get around to reading;  
* use ChatGPT or Claude to explain difficult concepts;  
* consume educational content while commuting, exercising, or walking;  
* value learning but struggle with attention and time.

They are already assembling the product manually from several different tools.

Our job is to collapse that workflow into one experience.

---

# **The product**

The product should initially be extremely simple.

### **Upload or open something.**

An EPUB.  
PDF.  
Article.  
Paper.  
Document.

Press:

**Continue Reading**

The system begins narrating.

At any point the user can say:

> “Pause.”

> “Explain that.”

> “What does that word mean?”

> “Give me an example.”

> “Go much deeper.”

> “What's the counterargument?”

> “How does this relate to the previous chapter?”

> “Explain it like I already understand machine learning.”

> “Okay, continue.”

The distinction that matters is that the AI does **not** optimize every response for conversational brevity.

When a question deserves a five-minute explanation, it can reason first, generate a substantive answer, and then narrate that answer beautifully.

**Depth should be a feature, not a failure of latency optimization.**

---

# **The larger vision**

The long-term product is not an AI audiobook reader.

It is a **personal intellectual companion**.

Over time it develops a model of:

### **What you know**

Which concepts you already understand.

### **What you have read**

Books, articles, papers, highlights, and conversations.

### **How you learn**

Whether you respond better to examples, analogies, first principles, historical context, mathematical explanations, or debate.

### **Where you struggle**

Concepts that repeatedly require clarification.

### **What you believe**

Not to reinforce those beliefs, but to understand where productive disagreement and counterarguments would help.

The system therefore becomes more valuable with every book.

When reading Nietzsche, it might say:

> “This is almost the opposite of the argument about suffering you discussed while reading Seneca six months ago.”

When reading an economics book:

> “This is the same mechanism you encountered in the Ruchir Sharma chapter about productivity.”

Knowledge stops being a collection of isolated books.

It becomes a connected graph of ideas built around the reader.

---

# **The moat**

Access to frontier models will not be a durable moat.

High-quality speech will not be a durable moat.

PDF parsing will not be a moat.

These technologies will commoditize.

Our moat must come from the accumulated **reader model and learning experience**.

Over time the product understands:

**What have you learned?**  
**What don't you understand?**  
**What have you forgotten?**  
**Which ideas connect?**  
**How should something be explained specifically to you?**

A new competitor may have the same underlying model.

It will not have years of accumulated context about the user's intellectual life.

That creates both personalization and switching cost.

This is not primarily:

**Text-to-speech.**

It is not:

**Chat with PDF.**

It is not:

**Audiobooks.**

It is not:

**AI summaries.**

And it is not exactly:

**AI tutoring.**

The emerging category is closer to:

## **Conversational Reading**

or

## **AI Reading Companion**

The defining interaction is:

> **Every piece of knowledge becomes something you can have a conversation with.**

---

# **What we believe will change**

Today, reading is predominantly:

**Author → Reader**

Audiobooks made it:

**Author → Listener**

Generative AI allows:

**Author ↔ Reader**

A book becomes less like a static object and more like an intellectual environment.

The reader can interrogate arguments, request context, challenge assumptions, explore tangents, and return to the original text.

This could meaningfully change how humans consume long-form knowledge.

---

# **What we must prove**

The biggest risk is not technological feasibility.

We already believe the product can be built.

The central question is behavioral:

> **Does conversational reading produce enough additional value that people choose it repeatedly over Kindle, Audible, podcasts, and ChatGPT?**

Specifically, we need to discover whether users:

1. finish more difficult books;  
2. understand them better;  
3. spend more time with long-form knowledge;  
4. repeatedly interrupt the AI to ask meaningful questions;  
5. return with their next book.

The strongest early signal would not be downloads.

It would be:

> **“I don't want to read my next difficult book without this.”**

---

# **The MVP**

The first version does not need a library, social network, marketplace, publishing ecosystem, or complicated learning system.

It needs to do one thing extraordinarily well:

> **Let someone upload a difficult book, listen to it, interrupt naturally with a question, receive an excellent deep explanation, and seamlessly continue.**

If we can make that experience feel magical, everything else can follow.

---

# **Founding conviction**

Humanity does not primarily suffer from a shortage of information.

We suffer from a shortage of **understanding, attention, and time**.

AI gives us the ability to place a patient, knowledgeable teacher beside every piece of text.

We believe that once people experience knowledge this way, a significant portion of long-form learning will move from passive reading toward **interactive, personalized conversation**.

**We want to build the interface through which that happens.**
